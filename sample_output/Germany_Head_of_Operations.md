## Caveats
- This seed brief reconstructs the Germany EU Blue Card research methodology from public government and named-research sources. It is not a copy of any private research memo and is not immigration, legal, or tax advice.
- Germany does not use US-style H-1B 'sponsorship.' The employer issues a qualifying contract; the candidate applies for the EU Blue Card. 'Sponsorship signal' here means public evidence of international professional hiring, not a visa lottery.
- Job-title ≠ ISCO code. A Head of Operations uses the reduced 2026 threshold (€45,934.20) only if duties fall in groups 132, 133 or 134 (or another listed group). A Product / ICT track is more often 133 or 25. Confirm classification with the employer and the Ausländerbehörde.
- Salary rows are official sector or occupation aggregates except the one row tagged SYNTHETIC. They are not an offer, a COO pay survey, or Glassdoor/Payscale scrapes.
- The 2026 Blue Card thresholds (€50,700 / €45,934.20) are reset annually in the Federal Gazette and on Make it in Germany. Re-check before any application.
- EY (€8.4bn, 716 rounds) and KfW/Dealroom (€7.2bn, 1,444 deals) are different VC coverage methods. Do not average them.
- Degree recognition (Anabin / ZAB) and regulated-profession licensing are out of scope of this run and can block an otherwise salary-qualified Blue Card.
- Figures accessed September 2026. Re-run live with an API key and optional MCP search before relying on them in a live search process.
